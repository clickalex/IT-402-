Chapter 13: Health, Safety and Security

🎯 Marks lens: Hazards, risk control, cyber safety, privacy and reporting are frequent case-study questions.

📖 Concepts explained simply

A hazard is something with potential to cause harm. Risk considers likelihood and consequence. Physical hazards include cables and poor posture; electrical hazards include damaged plugs; digital hazards include malware, phishing and unauthorised access. Prevention includes housekeeping, safe equipment, strong passwords, updates, backups and screen locking.

Term / featureMeaningExample
PhysicalTrip, posture or glareLoose cable
ElectricalShock or fireCracked plug
DigitalLoss or misuse of dataPhishing link
Real-life example: A clean desk prevents trips, a strong unique password reduces account risk and Win+L protects an unattended screen.

🖱️ Exact LibreOffice procedure

  1. Inspect cables, plugs, ventilation, spills and exits before work.
  2. Keep food and liquids away; do not use damaged equipment.
  3. Lock the computer with Win+L; use unique passwords and never share OTPs.
  4. Report a hazard to the supervisor, isolate danger if safe and record the action.

Useful keys: Win+L locks; Ctrl+S reduces work loss but is not a backup.

🧠 Memory trick: Spot, prevent, report.
⚠️ Common mistakes: Using a cracked plug; leaving accounts unlocked; opening suspicious attachments; posting private records online.

❓ One-mark questions

What is a hazard?
Something with the potential to cause harm.
Give one use of a hazard.
A loose cable across a walkway.
What is one benefit?
It improves consistency, accuracy or efficiency.
Name one precaution.
Check the result, save a version and protect private information.
Which skill does this test?
Correct terminology plus an application example.

Two-mark questions

Hazard versus risk?
A hazard is the source of possible harm; risk considers the likelihood and impact of that harm.
Give two digital-security controls.
Unique passwords, updates, backups, screen locking and careful links.
Why report near misses?
They reveal hazards before someone is injured or data is lost.

Four-mark model answer

Explain a computer-lab safety inspection.
Inspect physical equipment, cables, ventilation, lighting, exits and housekeeping. Check digital controls such as screen locks and updates. Remove or isolate hazards only if safe, alert the responsible person, document the issue and do not use unsafe equipment.

Chapter 14: Workplace Quality and Ergonomics

🎯 Marks lens: Posture, workstation layout, quality assurance/control and the 20-20-20 rule are high-value.

📖 Concepts explained simply

Ergonomics fits the work to the worker. Sit with back supported, shoulders relaxed, feet stable and wrists neutral. Keep the screen roughly 50–70 cm away, reduce glare and place the top near comfortable eye level. Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

Quality assurance improves the process; quality control checks the output. In data entry, validation rules, two-person review, duplicate checks and a sample audit reduce errors.

Term / featureMeaningExample
50–70 cmComfortable screen distanceAbout arm’s length
20-20-20Visual break20 min, 20 ft, 20 sec
QAPrevent process errorsEntry checklist
QCFind output errorsSample audit
Real-life example: An operator uses a checklist, validates phone numbers and takes visual breaks, reducing both incorrect records and neck strain.

🖱️ Exact LibreOffice procedure

  1. Place the screen about 50–70 cm away and reduce glare.
  2. Adjust chair and keyboard so back, elbows and wrists are supported.
  3. Use the 20-20-20 rule and change posture during long work.
  4. Create quality checks: mandatory fields, format validation, duplicate review and final sample audit.

Useful keys: 20-20-20; 50–70 cm.

🧠 Memory trick: Distance, posture, breaks, checks.
⚠️ Common mistakes: Bending wrists; screen glare; confusing QA with QC; treating speed as quality.

❓ One-mark questions

What is ergonomics?
The study of fitting work, tools and environment to the worker.
Give one use of ergonomics.
Adjusting chair and monitor to reduce strain.
What is one benefit?
It improves consistency, accuracy or efficiency.
Name one precaution.
Check the result, save a version and protect private information.
Which skill does this test?
Correct terminology plus an application example.

Two-mark questions

State the 20-20-20 rule.
Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
QA versus QC?
QA prevents process errors; QC detects errors in the finished output.
Give two data-entry quality checks.
Use mandatory fields and validation, then review duplicates or audit a sample.

Four-mark model answer

Explain an ergonomic and quality plan for data entry.
Set screen distance and height, support the back and wrists, reduce glare and take 20-20-20 breaks. Use a written process checklist, validation rules and a final sample audit. Record errors and improve the process rather than hiding them.

Chapter 15: Accidents and Emergencies

🎯 Marks lens: Response sequence, electrical/fire safety, evacuation and the numbers 101, 102 and 108 are examinable.

📖 Concepts explained simply

An accident is an unplanned event causing injury, damage or loss. In an emergency, stay calm and do not create a second victim. The general sequence is protect, alert, help/evacuate, report. Never touch a person in contact with live electricity until the supply is isolated safely. Do not use water on electrical fire.

Call the number and service taught by the school/curriculum: this revision guide uses 101 for police, 102 for ambulance and 108 for emergency response/ambulance. State your name, exact location, nature of emergency and hazards. Follow the evacuation route and assembly-point instructions.

Term / featureMeaningExample
ProtectMake scene saferKeep away from live wire
AlertCall responsible helpGive location
EvacuateUse safe routeAssembly point
Real-life example: During a fire alarm, the operator saves work only if there is no delay, switches off only when safe, evacuates without running and reports at the assembly point.

🖱️ Exact LibreOffice procedure

  1. Raise the alarm and alert the supervisor; give exact location and hazard.
  2. For electricity, isolate power only if safe and do not touch the person while live.
  3. For fire, evacuate; never use water on electrical equipment and use an extinguisher only if trained.
  4. Call 101 police, 102 ambulance or 108 emergency response/ambulance as taught; report missing people at the assembly point.

Useful keys: 101 police; 102 ambulance; 108 emergency response/ambulance as taught.

🧠 Memory trick: Protect → alert → help/evacuate → report.
⚠️ Common mistakes: Panic or crowding exits; returning for belongings; unclear location; untrained first aid or extinguisher use.

❓ One-mark questions

What is an accident?
An unplanned event that causes or could cause injury, damage or loss.
Give one use of an accident.
A person trips over an exposed cable.
What is one benefit?
It improves consistency, accuracy or efficiency.
Name one precaution.
Check the result, save a version and protect private information.
Which skill does this test?
Correct terminology plus an application example.

Two-mark questions

Give two details when calling emergency help.
Exact location and nature of emergency; also state injuries, hazards and callback details if asked.
What must you not do in an electrical accident?
Do not touch the person while electricity may be live and do not use water.
Why is an assembly point needed?
It allows evacuation accountability and prevents people returning into danger.

Four-mark model answer

Write the correct response to an electrical fire.
Raise the alarm and alert the supervisor. Keep people away and isolate power only if safe; never touch a victim while live and never use water. Evacuate by the safe route, call the appropriate service with the location and report missing people at the assembly point.
