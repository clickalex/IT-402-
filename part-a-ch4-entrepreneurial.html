Chapter 4: Entrepreneurial Skills-II

Spot a customer need, organise resources, take calculated risks and create value — ethically.

📖 Part 1 · Detailed chapter

🎯 Marks lens (3 marks): qualities/functions, myths vs facts, entrepreneurship as career, one value-proposition or business-plan outline. Use revenue − cost = profit correctly.

1. Entrepreneurship and society

An entrepreneur identifies an opportunity, organises resources and accepts calculated risk to create value. Society gains jobs, better services, competition and local problem-solving.

Local example: shops can't find old paper bills → a student team offers digitising + validation + searchable backup as a paid weekend service.

2. Qualities and functions of an entrepreneur

QualityIn action
InitiativeStarts without being pushed
CreativityFinds a simpler/better way
PersistenceContinues after rejection
Problem-solvingBreaks issues into fixable parts
CommunicationClear promises + honest updates
Risk managementIdentifies risk, reduces it, keeps backup

Functions: spot opportunity → gather resources → plan finance → deliver quality → handle risk → collect feedback → improve.

3. Myths vs facts

Myth ❌Fact ✅
"Entrepreneurs are born, not made."Skills like planning, costing and selling can be learned and practised.
"You need a lot of money to start."Many services start small — one computer + skill + trust.
"Risk means guessing."Professionals take calculated risks with data and backups.
"Profit is the only goal."Trust, quality and repeat customers sustain profit.

4. Entrepreneurship as a career option + mini plan

  1. Customer + problem in one line: "Nearby shops lose time finding paper bills."
  2. Service + value proposition: "We digitise 1 month of bills in 3 days with validated search."
  3. Resources: 2 students, 1 laptop, LibreOffice, 6 hours, travel cost.
  4. Money: cost → price → profit (e.g. ₹600 cost, ₹1,000 price = ₹400 profit).
  5. Risks + controls: privacy leak → written consent + restricted access; wrong totals → double-check + sample audit.
  6. Test: one pilot shop, collect feedback, then decide to scale.
🧠 Memory trick: Need → Idea → Resources → Risk → Value. Money: R − C = P.
⚠️ Common mistakes: calling revenue "profit"; promising impossible delivery; using customer records without permission; ignoring backup/privacy; no pilot test.

One-mark questions

1. Who is an entrepreneur?
A person who identifies an opportunity, organises resources and accepts calculated risk to create value.
2. Revenue vs profit?
Revenue is money received; profit is revenue minus cost.
3. What is a value proposition?
A clear statement of the customer problem solved and the benefit offered.
4. Name two entrepreneurial qualities.
Initiative, creativity, persistence, problem-solving, communication (any two).
5. Give one myth about entrepreneurship.
"Entrepreneurs are born" / "need huge money" / "risk means guessing" (any one + correction).
6. What is calculated risk?
Risk taken after identifying it, estimating loss and planning controls/backups.

Two-mark questions

1. How does entrepreneurship help society? (2 points)
Creates jobs and better local services; solves real problems through competition and innovation.
2. Correct this myth: "Profit is the only goal."
Profit matters, but trust, quality and repeat customers sustain it; unethical shortcuts destroy it.
3. Give one risk + control for a data service.
Privacy leak → written consent, restricted access, no copies kept after delivery.
4. What is a pilot test?
Trying the service with one customer first to collect feedback before scaling.

Four-mark model answers

Plan an ethical data-entry service for local shops. (4)
Need: shops can't find paper bills. Service: digitise + validate + searchable backup in 3 days. Resources: team, laptop, LibreOffice, hours. Money: cost ₹600, price ₹1,000, profit ₹400. Risks: privacy/wrong totals → consent, restricted access, double-check, sample audit. Test with one pilot shop and improve from feedback.
Explain qualities of a successful entrepreneur with examples. (4)
Initiative (starts pilot without push), persistence (continues after one rejection), problem-solving (splits "messy bills" into sort→enter→validate), communication (clear promise + honest updates) and risk management (backup + consent). Each quality converts directly into customer trust and repeat work.
