Chapter 2: Self-Management Skills-II

Plan work, control stress, stay motivated and deliver accurate data entry — with SMART goals and time planning.

📖 Part 1 · Detailed chapter

🎯 Marks lens (3 marks — highest in Part A with Unit 4): stress management, self-awareness, self-motivation, SMART goals, time management. Expect applied 2–4 mark cases.

1. Stress management

Stress is the body's reaction when demands feel bigger than our ability to cope. Short stress can alert us; long, uncontrolled stress harms health and accuracy.

Symptom groupSignsHealthy response
BodyHeadache, tired eyes, stiff neckShort breaks, stretch, water, sleep
MindWorry, forgetting steps, rushingBreak task into batches, checklist
BehaviourSkipping checks, hiding errorsReport early, ask for help
Operator case: 200 records due today → split into 4 batches of 50, 5-min break between batches, quality check after each batch.

2. Self-awareness: know your strengths & weaknesses

Self-awareness means honestly judging what you do well and where you need support — then planning around it.

✅ Strength examples
Fast typing, careful checking, polite customer talk.
Use: take accuracy-critical batches.
🔧 Weakness → plan
Slow with formulas → practise 15 min daily.
Distracted by phone → keep it away during batches.

3. Self-motivation (internal + external)

TypeMeaningExample
InternalSatisfaction from good work itself"Zero-error batch feels good."
ExternalReward/praise from othersTeacher appreciation, certificate

Internal motivation lasts longer. Build it with small wins: finish batch 1 cleanly → confidence for batch 2.

4. SMART goals + self-regulation

LetterMeansWeak goal ❌SMART goal ✅
SpecificExactly what"Do data entry""Enter 20 student records"
MeasurableCountable proof"Do well""Zero blank mandatory fields"
AchievablePossible now"500 in 1 hour""20 in 50 minutes"
RelevantMatches the real target"Learn games""Complete today's batch"
Time-boundDeadline"Sometime""By 4 pm"

Self-regulation = controlling distractions and feelings to stay on the goal: silence notifications, follow the checklist, take planned breaks.

5. Time management & independent work

  1. List tasks; mark urgent (fix wrong record now) vs important (daily backup).
  2. Estimate time per batch; add buffer for checking.
  3. Batch the work: 50 entries → check → save version → break.
  4. Review error count; note what went wrong; improve next batch.
🧠 Memory trick: SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Plan → Batch → Check → Break.
⚠️ Common mistakes: unrealistic deadlines; multitasking during entry; hiding errors; skipping breaks; confusing urgent with important.

One-mark questions

1. What is self-management?
The ability to plan work, control distractions, use time wisely and take responsibility for results.
2. Expand SMART.
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
3. What is self-motivation?
The internal willingness to begin and continue a task without constant supervision.
4. Give one physical symptom of stress.
Headache, eye strain, stiff neck or tiredness.
5. Urgent vs important — one example of each?
Urgent: fix a wrong record now. Important: daily backup.
6. What is self-regulation?
Controlling one's behaviour, distractions and emotions to stay on a goal.

Two-mark questions

1. Give two time-management techniques.
Prioritise urgent vs important; divide large work into timed batches with breaks and checks.
2. Name two healthy stress controls.
Plan realistically; take short breaks; stretch; sleep well; talk to a trusted person.
3. Internal vs external motivation?
Internal comes from satisfaction in the work itself; external comes from rewards or praise.
4. Why should errors be reported early?
Early reporting limits damage, allows correction and builds trust; hiding errors multiplies them.

Four-mark model answers

Your teacher gives 200 records due tomorrow. Explain your plan. (4)
Clarify output + deadline and set a SMART target (e.g. 50/batch). Split into 4 batches with time estimates. Remove distractions, use a checklist (mandatory fields, formats), save versions and check error count after each batch. Take short breaks; report blockers early instead of hiding them.
Explain self-awareness with strengths/weaknesses. (4)
Self-awareness is honest judgement of one's abilities. List strengths (e.g. careful checking) and use them for accuracy-critical work. List weaknesses (e.g. slow formulas) and convert each into an action (15-min daily practice). Review weekly: what improved, what still needs support — and ask for help where needed.

💻 Part 3 · Practice

  1. SMART rewrite: convert "I will study computers" into a SMART goal for this week.
  2. Strength/weakness sheet: list 3 strengths + 2 weaknesses and one action for each weakness.
  3. 60-minute plan: on paper, plan two data batches + quality check + backup + break with exact timings.
  4. Stress log: for 3 days note one stress moment, its symptom and the healthy response you used.
