# Unit 1: Communication Skills-II

## 🎯 Why it matters
Practise the 7 Cs, barriers and feedback using workplace examples. The published 2026–27 scheme allocates 2 marks to this unit within the 10-mark Part A total; this does not guarantee a particular question.

## 📖 Concepts (deep)

**1. What is communication?** Transfer of information/ideas/feelings between sender and receiver through a medium, with feedback completing the loop.

**2. Communication cycle (7 steps) — draw this in 2-marker:**
Sender → **Encoding** (converting thought into words/gestures) → **Message** → **Channel/Medium** (phone, letter, face-to-face) → **Decoding** (receiver interprets) → **Receiver** → **Feedback** (reply/confirmation).
*Example:* Teacher explains (sender/encoding) → speaks in class (message/channel) → student understands (decoding) → nods/asks doubt (feedback).

**3. Methods of communication:**
| Method | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal – Oral | Spoken words | Phone call, speech, meeting |
| Verbal – Written | Written words | Email, letter, report, SMS |
| Non-verbal | Body, voice, touch | Smile, handshake, eye contact, tone, posture |
| Visual | Pictures/signs | Charts, maps, traffic signals, logos |

**4. 7 Cs of effective communication (memorise with example):**
1. **Clear** – simple words, one idea at a time (“Submit file by Friday” not “do it soon-ish”)
2. **Concise** – short, no extra words
3. **Concrete** – specific facts/figures (“sales rose 20%” not “sales rose a lot”)
4. **Correct** – accurate grammar, facts, spelling
5. **Coherent** – logical order, connected sentences
6. **Complete** – all needed info (what, when, where, whom)
7. **Courteous** – polite, respectful (“please”, “thank you”)

**5. Barriers (with one-line example each):**
- **Physical** – noise, distance, faulty phone
- **Linguistic/Language** – different languages, jargon (“client speaks French, manual in English”)
- **Cultural** – different customs/gestures meanings
- **Emotional** – anger/fear blocks listening
- **Perceptual** – different viewpoints/assumptions
- **Organisational** – strict hierarchy, no feedback channel
- **Interpersonal** – poor relationship, distrust, interruptions

**6. Feedback:** Receiver's response showing message was understood. Good feedback is **descriptive** (says what happened), **specific**, **timely**, **constructive** (suggests improvement, not insults).
*Example:* ❌ “Bad presentation.” ✅ “Your data was good; add bigger fonts on slide 3 and slow down.”

**7. Principles of effective communication:** Clarity, completeness, attention (listen fully), consistency (words match actions), timeliness, proper channel, feedback, courtesy.

**8. Speaking skills:** Correct grammar, clear pronunciation, fluency (smooth flow), rich vocabulary, right speed + voice modulation (stress/pause/tone).

**9. Overcoming stage fear (public speaking):** Know your topic, prepare notes, practise aloud/in mirror, deep breathing, start with smile + greeting, make eye contact, use simple slides, interact (ask questions).

**10. Active listening (DOs):** Face speaker, eye contact, nod, don't interrupt, paraphrase (“So you mean…?”), take notes, ask clarifying questions.

## 🧠 Memory tricks
- 7 Cs → **"Can Cows Chew Cold Corn Cob Candy?"** (Clear Concise Concrete Correct Coherent Complete Courteous)
- Cycle order → **SEMC DRF** (“Seema Drink RF”): Sender-Encoding-Message-Channel-Decoding-Receiver-Feedback

## ⚠️ Mistakes to avoid
- Writing only “verbal/non-verbal” without oral/written split when asked for methods.
- Confusing Encoding (sender side) vs Decoding (receiver side).
- Forgetting example in 2-marker — example = half marks.

## ❓ Exam Q&A
**1-mark:** “French manual” barrier → Linguistic. “7th C starting with Co…” → Courteous/Coherent/Concrete/Complete/Correct (any).
**2-mark Q:** *Explain any two Cs.* → Pick Clear + Concise/Courteous, 1 line + 1 mini-example each.
**2-mark Q:** *What is feedback? Why important?* → Response showing understanding; confirms message, corrects errors, improves performance; must be specific + timely + constructive.

## 💻 Practical (for file/viva)
Write a formal email to principal requesting leave (Subject + greeting + 3-line body + sign-off). Demonstrates written communication + email etiquette.

# Unit 2: Self-Management Skills-II

## 🎯 Why it matters
Practise stress-management techniques and SMART goals for objective and two-mark answers. Topic recurrence in sample papers is not a guarantee of questions or marks.

## 📖 Concepts (deep)

**1. Self-management =** Managing yourself — emotions, time, goals, habits — to be productive and balanced. Three pillars: **self-awareness** (know strengths/weaknesses/values), **self-regulation** (control reactions), **self-motivation** (inner drive).

**2. Stress:** Body/mind pressure from demanding situations. Small stress motivates; excess harms.
- **Causes:** Exams/deadlines, overload, change (new school), conflicts, noise/heat, poor sleep, social media pressure.
- **Effects:** Headache, insomnia, fast heartbeat, anxiety, anger, poor concentration, low marks.
- **Management techniques (learn any 4 with 1 line):**
  1. Time management (plan, prioritise, to-do list)
  2. Exercise/yoga/meditation + deep breathing
  3. 7–8 hrs sleep + healthy food + less junk
  4. Talk to family/friends/teacher (share feelings)
  5. Short breaks + hobbies/music/walk
  6. Positive thinking + realistic goals (don't compare)
  7. Say NO politely to overload

**3. Goal setting – SMART (expand + example):**
| Letter | Means | Example (board prep) |
|---|---|---|
| S Specific | Exact goal | “Finish DBMS Ch8–9” not “study IT” |
| M Measurable | Can count it | “20 MCQs + 2 long answers” |
| A Achievable | Possible | 2 hrs/day, not 10 hrs suddenly |
| R Realistic/Relevant | Matches need | IT ch. before pre-board date |
| T Time-bound | Deadline | “By Sunday 6 pm” |

**4. Time management:** To-do list daily → prioritise (Urgent+Important first) → fix time slots → avoid procrastination (“do 25-min pomodoro, then 5-min break”) → review at night. Benefits: less stress, better quality, meets deadlines.

**5. Personality (Big Five – OCEAN):** Openness (creative/curious), Conscientiousness (disciplined), Extraversion (social/talkative), Agreeableness (helpful/kind), Neuroticism (anxious/moody). No good/bad — awareness helps choose roles (e.g. extravert → sales, conscientious → data entry).

**6. Grooming & hygiene:** Bath daily, neat uniform/hair/nails, clean shoes, good posture, polite body language. Builds confidence + first impression for interviews/jobs.

**7. Self-motivation builders:** Positive self-talk (“I can improve 5 marks”), reward small wins, inspiring company, track progress chart, learn from failure (analyse, don't quit).

## 🧠 Memory tricks
- SMART → **“Smart Monkeys Achieve Really Tough-goals”**
- OCEAN = Big Five order itself. Stress-busters → **“TEB-Talk-Break”**: Time-plan, Exercise, Balanced-sleep/food, Talk, Breaks.

## ⚠️ Mistakes to avoid
- Writing “SMART = good goals” without expanding each letter — zero marks. Always expand all 5.
- Only listing stress effects without techniques when Q asks “suggest measures”.

## ❓ Exam Q&A
**1-mark:** “T in SMART?” → Time-bound. “Big Five trait for disciplined person?” → Conscientiousness.
**2-mark Q:** *Maya gets frustrated, reacts impulsively. Suggest two techniques.* → (1) Pause + deep breathing/count 10 before reacting (self-regulation). (2) Prioritise with to-do list + short breaks to cut overload.
**2-mark Q:** *Two benefits of time management.* → Finishes work on time with less stress; improves quality + leaves time for revision/hobbies.

## 💻 Practical
Make a 1-week SMART study timetable in Calc (Goal / Measurable target / Deadline columns) + tick daily. Paste in file as self-management evidence.

# Unit 3: ICT Skills-II

## 🎯 Why it matters
Explain operating system functions, file care, maintenance and safe access. Prepare both objectives and short answers; do not assume this unit is limited to one mark.

## 📖 Concepts (deep)

**1. ICT =** Technologies for creating, storing, sharing information + communicating (computer, internet, phone, email).
**2. Computer system:** Hardware (you touch: keyboard, CPU, monitor, printer) + Software (programs). Software = **System** (OS, antivirus, drivers) + **Application** (Writer, Calc, browser, games).
**3. Operating System (OS):** Master software; interface between user & hardware; manages files, memory, devices, users. Examples: Windows 11, Ubuntu/Linux, macOS, Android. Functions: file management, process/memory/device management, security (passwords), provides UI (GUI/CLI).
**4. File management (must-know operations):** Create folder/file, rename (F2), copy (Ctrl+C)/cut/move, paste (Ctrl+V), delete (Del) + Restore from Recycle Bin/Trash, search by name, sort by date/size, check extension (.odt Writer, .ods Calc, .odb Base, .jpg image). Good habits: meaningful names (`IT-Ch8-Notes.odt`), subject-wise folders, no desktop dumping.
**5. Computer maintenance (any 4):** Disk Cleanup + delete temp files, update OS/software, update + scan antivirus weekly, uninstall unused apps, defragment HDD (not SSD), clean keyboard/screen safely (switched off), proper shutdown (not power cut), regular **backup** (external drive/cloud).
**6. Security & safety:** Long, unique password or passphrase for each account; enable multi-factor authentication where available, firewall ON, antivirus updated, lock screen (Win+L), log out on shared PCs, don't click unknown links/attachments, check the website address carefully (HTTPS encrypts the connection but does not prove a site is trustworthy), never share OTP/password, backup important data, avoid pirated software.
**7. Email etiquette:** Clear subject (“Leave application – 12 Sept”), polite greeting, short paragraphs, attach before sending + mention attachment, proofread, formal sign-off (Name, Class, Roll No).

## 🧠 Memory tricks
- Extensions → **“Writers Type Documents (.odt), Calculators Sum (.ods), Base Stores (.odb)”**
- Safety → **“PAF-BL”**: Password-strong, Antivirus, Firewall, Backup, Lock/Log-out.

## ⚠️ Mistakes to avoid
- Calling LibreOffice Writer an “OS” — it's application software. OS = Windows/Linux.
- Forgetting backup in maintenance answers — backup is almost always in marking scheme.

## ❓ Exam Q&A
**1-mark:** “.ods belongs to?” → LibreOffice Calc. “Win+L does?” → Locks screen.
**2-mark Q:** *Two maintenance habits for smooth PC.* → Weekly antivirus update+scan and disk cleanup/temp deletion; monthly backup + OS/software updates + proper shutdown.

# Unit 4: Entrepreneurial Skills-II

## 🎯 Why it matters
Explain entrepreneur vs employee, entrepreneurial qualities, functions and myths with examples. Prepare the whole unit rather than predicting particular questions.

## 📖 Concepts (deep)

**1. Definitions:** **Entrepreneur** = person who starts/runs a business, takes risk, innovates for profit (e.g. local bakery owner, startup founder). **Entrepreneurship** = the process (idea → plan → launch → manage → grow). **Enterprise/Business** = the venture itself.

**2. Entrepreneur vs Employee (exam table — learn 3 rows):**
| Basis | Entrepreneur | Employee |
|---|---|---|
| Risk & income | Bears risk; uncertain profit/loss | No business risk; fixed salary |
| Decisions | Takes own decisions | Follows employer's instructions |
| Work | Own idea, flexible hours, more responsibility | Fixed role/hours, less responsibility |
| Motive | Build + grow venture | Career growth + stable income |

**3. Qualities of successful entrepreneur (learn 6 + 1 line):** Risk-taking (calculated), leadership (guides team), decision-making (quick + firm), hard work + persistence (don't quit), innovation/creativity (new ideas), communication (convinces customers/team), goal-oriented, adaptable, honest, confident.

**4. Functions of entrepreneur:** Generate idea → survey market → prepare business plan → arrange money/material/men → launch → market & sell → manage accounts → bear risk → grow/innovate.

**5. Myths vs Facts (very asked):**
- Myth: Entrepreneurs are born → Fact: skills can be learned/practised.
- Myth: Need lots of money → Fact: can start small (home/cloud kitchen, tuition, online store).
- Myth: Only for business families / only men / needs big degree → Fact: anyone with idea + effort; many first-generation + women founders.

**6. Steps to start a business (6):** Idea → Market survey (customers? competitors? price?) → Business plan (product, customers, cost, price, marketing, finance) → Arrange finance (savings/loan/scheme) → Set up + register (licence/shop) → Launch → Manage, review, grow.

**7. Business plan contents:** What product/service, who customers, competitor analysis, pricing, marketing (how to reach buyers), money needed + profit estimate, daily operations, risks + backup.

**8. Risk management:** Identify risks (fire, theft, no sales) → analyse impact → reduce: insurance, backup stock/cash, quality control, diversify products, written agreements.

**9. Financial literacy basics:** Budget (income − expense), save first, track profit = selling price − cost, avoid over-borrowing, keep bills/records, separate business + personal money.

## 🧠 Memory tricks
- Qualities → **“RLD-HIC”**: Risk, Leadership, Decision, Hard-work, Innovation, Communication.
- Steps → **“I-M-P-F-S-L”**: Idea, Market-survey, Plan, Finance, Setup, Launch.

## ⚠️ Mistakes to avoid
- One-sided difference (only entrepreneur's side). Always write both columns.
- Vague “takes risk” without contrasting “fixed salary” — pair both for full marks.

## ❓ Exam Q&A
**1-mark:** “Fixed salary person is?” → Employee. “Part of business plan?” → Pricing/marketing/finance (any).
**2-mark Q:** *Two qualities of entrepreneur.* → Risk-taking: invests despite uncertainty (opens cloud kitchen). Communication: convinces customers/suppliers clearly.
**2-mark Q:** *Two myths with facts.* → (1) Born entrepreneurs — false, skills learnable. (2) Needs huge money — false, can start small/home-based.

# Unit 5: Green Skills-II

## 🎯 Why it matters
Explain sustainable development and green jobs, then apply resource-saving actions to school or workplace examples. Questions and marks are not fixed to one definition.

## 📖 Concepts (deep)

**1. Environment =** Our surroundings: air, water, land, plants, animals + human society. Damage = pollution, deforestation, waste, climate change.

**2. Sustainable Development (Brundtland, 1987 — memorise exact line):** *“Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.”*
**Three pillars:** Environment (protect nature) + Society (health, equity) + Economy (jobs, growth) — all three together.

**3. Green Economy:** Economic growth that is low-carbon, resource-efficient, pollution-free, socially inclusive. E.g. solar energy, e-vehicles, recycling industry, organic farming.

**4. Green Jobs:** Jobs protecting/restoring environment. Examples: solar panel technician, wind-turbine mechanic, energy auditor, waste-segregation manager, water-harvesting expert, organic farmer, e-vehicle mechanic, forest guard. (Non-green contrast: plastic-bag factory worker.)

**5. Green Skills:** Skills for green jobs — install solar panels, audit energy, manage e-waste, compost wet waste, harvest rainwater, design eco-packaging.

**6. 4 Rs:** **Refuse** (say no to plastic), **Reduce** (less use), **Reuse** (bottles/bags), **Recycle** (paper/e-waste to recycler).

**7. School/home actions (any 4 for 2-marker):** LED bulbs + switch off; save water (fix leaks, bucket bath); segregate wet/dry/e-waste bins; compost leaves/food waste; plant + maintain trees; cycle/walk/bus; double-side printing; e-notes over photocopies; clean + green drives; World Environment Day 5 June activities.

## 🧠 Memory tricks
- Pillars → **“ESE”**: Environment-Society-Economy (“Easy”).
- 4 Rs order → **Refuse-Reduce-Reuse-Recycle** (strongest → weakest action).

## ❓ Exam Q&A
**1-mark:** “Brundtland defines?” → Sustainable development. “Green job example?” → Solar technician.
**2-mark Q:** *What is green economy? Give two green jobs.* → Low-carbon, resource-efficient, inclusive growth model. Jobs: solar technician + e-waste manager (any valid pair).
