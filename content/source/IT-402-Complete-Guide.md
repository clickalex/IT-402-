## PART A – EMPLOYABILITY SKILLS (10 marks)

### Unit 1: Communication Skills-II (2 marks)

**Communication cycle:** Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback.
**Methods:** Verbal (oral/written), Non-verbal (body language, gestures, eye contact, posture, tone), Visual (charts, signs, images).
**7 Cs of effective communication:** Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous.
**Barriers:** Physical, Linguistic/language, Cultural, Emotional, Perceptual, Organisational, Interpersonal.
**Feedback:** Descriptive (what happened), Specific, Timely; helps improve performance. Always constructive, not hurtful.
**Principles of effective communication:** Clarity, attention, consistency, timeliness, feedback, active listening.
**Speaking skills:** Grammar, pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary, voice modulation.
**Public speaking fear:** Prepare well, practise, deep breathing, know audience, start simple.
**Active listening:** Focus, nod/eye-contact, don’t interrupt, paraphrase to confirm.

> Typical 1-marker: “Which barrier? Different languages” → Linguistic barrier. “7 Cs” match type is very common.

### Unit 2: Self-Management Skills-II (3 marks – highest weight in Part A)

**Stress:** Pressure/tension from demanding situations. **Stress management** = handling stress positively.
**Causes of stress:** Work overload, deadlines, exams, relationships, change, environment.
**Effects:** Headache, lack of sleep, anxiety, poor concentration, irritability.
**Stress management techniques:** Time management, exercise/yoga/meditation, healthy sleep + diet, talking to friends/family, prioritising, taking breaks, positive thinking.
**Self-awareness:** Knowing your strengths, weaknesses, values, emotions.
**Self-regulation:** Controlling emotions and behaviour. **Self-motivation:** Inner drive to achieve goals.
**Goal setting – SMART goals:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic/Relevant, Time-bound.
**Time management:** To-do list, prioritise (urgent vs important), avoid procrastination, set deadlines, say no politely.
**Personality traits (Big Five – OCEAN):** Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
**Grooming:** Personal hygiene, neat dress, posture — builds confidence and employability.

> Typical 2-marker: “Maya gets frustrated and reacts impulsively. Suggest two self-management techniques.” → deep breathing / pause before reacting + time management / prioritising tasks.

### Unit 3: ICT Skills-II (1 mark)

**ICT = Information and Communication Technology.** Used for storing, retrieving, communicating information.
**Computer system:** Hardware (input/process/output/storage) + Software (system + application).
**Operating System:** Interface between user and hardware; manages files, memory, devices. E.g. Windows, Linux, Ubuntu.
**File management:** Create/rename/copy/move/delete files & folders; file extension shows type (.odt, .ods, .odb).
**Maintenance:** Disk cleanup, antivirus update + scan, delete temp files, regular backup, update software/OS.
**Security:** Strong passwords, firewall, antivirus, don’t click unknown links, log out, backup data.
**Internet safety:** No sharing OTP/passwords, verify websites (https), avoid piracy, respect privacy.
**Email etiquette:** Clear subject, polite greeting, short body, attach carefully, sign off.

> Only 1 mark in theory but asked almost every year — usually OS/file-maintenance or cyber-safety MCQ.

### Unit 4: Entrepreneurial Skills-II (3 marks)

**Entrepreneur:** Person who starts and runs a business, taking risk for profit. **Entrepreneurship:** Process of setting up and managing a venture.
**Entrepreneur vs Employee:** Entrepreneur takes risk + makes decisions + earns profit (uncertain); employee gets fixed salary + follows instructions.
**Qualities of successful entrepreneur:** Risk-taking, leadership, decision-making, hard work, innovation/creativity, communication, persistence, goal-oriented, adaptability.
**Functions:** Idea generation, planning, organising resources, managing, marketing, bearing risk.
**Myths:** Entrepreneurs are born (❌ — skills can be learned); need lots of money (❌ — can start small); only for business families (❌).
**Steps to start a business:** Idea → Market survey → Business plan → Arrange finance → Set up → Launch → Manage & grow.
**Business plan includes:** Product/service, customers, competition, pricing, marketing, finance, operations.
**Risk management:** Identify risk → analyse → reduce (insurance, backup plans, diversification).
**Financial literacy:** Budgeting, saving, understanding profit/loss, basic accounting.

> Favourite 2-marker: “Differentiate entrepreneur and employee (any 2 points)” or “qualities of an entrepreneur”.

### Unit 5: Green Skills-II (1 mark)

**Environment:** Surroundings (air, water, land, living beings).
**Sustainable development:** Development that meets present needs without harming future generations’ ability to meet theirs. (Brundtland definition — often asked.)
**Three pillars:** Environment + Society + Economy.
**Green economy:** Economy that is low-carbon, resource-efficient, socially inclusive; reduces pollution.
**Green jobs:** Jobs that protect/restore the environment. E.g. solar technician, waste manager, organic farmer, e-vehicle mechanic, energy auditor.
**Green skills:** Skills needed for green jobs — e.g. rainwater harvesting, waste segregation, energy conservation.
**Conservation at home/school:** Save electricity (LED, switch off), save water, reduce-reuse-recycle, say no to plastic, plant trees, use public transport/cycle.
**Important days:** World Environment Day – 5 June.

> Almost fixed 1-marker: definition of sustainable development or example of green job.

## PART B – SUBJECT SKILLS (40 marks)

# UNIT 1: Digital Documentation (Advanced) – LibreOffice Writer | 8 marks

## Chapter 1: Introduction to Styles

**Style =** A saved set of formatting (font, size, colour, spacing, alignment) that can be applied in one click. Ensures consistency in long documents.

**6 Style categories in Writer:**
1. Page – page size, margins, header/footer, background
2. Paragraph – font + paragraph formatting together (most used; e.g. Heading 1, Text Body)
3. Character – only selected characters (e.g. bold+red for keywords)
4. Frame – frames/text boxes containing images/objects
5. List – bullets and numbering formats
6. Table – table borders, shading, alignment

**Styles and Formatting window:** Press **F11** or Sidebar → Styles. Shows all styles; double-click to apply.

**Fill Format Mode:** Paintbrush icon in Styles window. Click it, then click/drag over paragraphs to “paint” the selected style on multiple areas quickly. Press Esc to exit.

**Creating a new style – 2 methods:**
1. **From Selection:** Format paragraph as desired → open Styles (F11) → click New Style from Selection icon → give name → OK.
2. **Drag and Drop:** Select formatted text → drag and drop it into the Styles window → type style name → OK.

**Updating a style:** Modify a paragraph that uses the style → in Styles window, right-click the style → **Update Selected Style** (or click Update icon). Whole document updates wherever that style was used.

**Load styles from template/document:** Styles window → New Style from Selection dropdown → **Load Styles from Template** → select file → choose style types (Text/Frame/Pages/Lists) → OK.

**Applying styles:** Select text/paragraph → double-click style in F11 window. (Paragraph: click anywhere in para; Character: select exact text first.)

## Chapter 2: Working with Images

**4 ways to insert an image:**
1. **Insert Image option:** Insert → Image → From File → select → Open
2. **Drag and Drop:** Drag image file from file manager into document
3. **Copy and Paste:** Ctrl+C image, Ctrl+V in document
4. **Linking:** Insert → Image → check **Link** option – image stays outside; document only stores path, so file size stays small. If source image moves/deletes, link breaks.

**Modify using Image toolbar:** Resize (drag corner handles — Shift+drag keeps ratio), **Crop** (cut unwanted edges), Delete (select + Delete key), plus brightness/contrast, wrap, border, filters.

**Drawing Objects:** Shapes like rectangle, circle, line, arrows, flowchart symbols. Insert via View → Toolbars → Drawing, or Insert → Shape. Each object has **properties:** line colour/style/width, area/fill colour, shadow, position-size.
**Resize & Group:** Select one object → drag handles to resize. Select multiple (Shift+click) → right-click → **Group** – they move/resize as one unit. Ungroup to edit separately.

**Positioning images in text:**
- **Arrangement:** Order of overlapping objects – Bring to Front / Forward / Backward / Send to Back.
- **Anchoring (very important):** What the image is “tied” to –
  - *To Page:* stays fixed on that page even if text moves
  - *To Paragraph:* moves with that paragraph
  - *To Character:* attached to a character
  - *As Character:* behaves like a text character in the line
- **Alignment:** Left / Center / Right / Justified position on page.
- **Text Wrapping:** How text flows around image – Page Wrap, Optimal Page Wrap, Wrap Left, Wrap Right, Wrap Through (text over image), In Background, Contour (follows shape edge), No Wrap.

## Chapter 3: Advanced Features of Writer

### (a) Table of Contents (ToC)
**Hierarchy of headings:** Use Heading 1 for chapter titles, Heading 2 for sections, Heading 3 for sub-sections. ToC is auto-built from these styles.
**Creating ToC:** Apply heading styles first → place cursor where ToC needed → Insert → Table of Contents and Index → Table of Contents, Index or Bibliography → OK.
**Customisation:** In the ToC dialog – change title, add levels (up to 10), set tab/leader dots, include/exclude outline levels, set background/borders.
**Maintaining ToC:** After editing document – right-click ToC → **Update Index** (updates page numbers/entries); **Delete Index** removes it. Never type ToC manually.

### (b) Templates
**Template =** A pre-designed model document (.ott) with fixed styles, layout, header/footer – used to create similar documents fast (resume, letter, report).
**Using:** File → New → Templates → pick in-built/saved/online template → Open. Or File → Templates → Manage Templates.
**Creating:** Design document → File → Templates → **Save as Template** → name + category → Save.
**Importing:** Manage Templates → Import → select .ott file.
**Editing:** Manage Templates → right-click template → Edit → change → Save (future documents use new design; old ones unchanged unless updated).
**Moving/Exporting:** Manage Templates → right-click → Move to folder / **Export** (to share .ott file with others).
**Applying to blank document:** Open blank doc → File → Templates → Manage Templates → double-click template (styles get applied) — or File → New from Template.

### (c) Track Changes
**Feature:** Records every insertion/deletion/formatting so reviewers can see and accept/reject edits. Used in collaboration.
**Prepare document for review:** Save a copy (File → Save As); then Edit → Track Changes → **Record** (or Ctrl+Shift+E). Share this copy.
**Recording:** With Record ON, deleted text shows as strikethrough, added text underlined/coloured, with author name in margin.
**Accept/Reject:** Edit → Track Changes → **Manage** → select each change → Accept or Reject (or Accept All / Reject All).
**Comments:** Select text → Insert → Comment (Ctrl+Alt+C) → type suggestion. Right-click comment → Delete (or Delete All). Comments don’t change text, only advise.
**Compare Documents:** Edit → Track Changes → **Compare Document** → select original file → differences open as tracked changes in a merged view.

> ⭐ Most-asked Unit 1 Qs: style categories; Fill Format; new style 2 methods; anchoring options; wrapping options; ToC steps; template vs document; Track Changes steps; comments shortcut.

# UNIT 2: Electronic Spreadsheet (Advanced) – LibreOffice Calc | 10 marks

## Chapter 4: Analyse Data using Scenarios and Goal Seek

**Consolidating Data:** Combining data from multiple ranges/sheets/files into one summary. Data → **Consolidate** → choose Function (SUM/AVERAGE/COUNT…), select Source ranges → Add → select target cell → OK. E.g. branch-wise sales → total sales sheet.

**Groups and Subtotals:** **Group** (Data → Group) collapses/expands rows/columns with +/− buttons for cleaner view. **Subtotals:** Data must be **sorted** first by the grouping column → Data → **Subtotals** → Group by column → Function (SUM/COUNT…) → columns to total → OK. Automatically inserts SUBTOTAL() rows. Max 3 nested levels; Remove via Data → Subtotals → Remove.

**What-if Analysis:** Predicting results by changing input values. E.g. “What if price rises 10%?”
**What-if Scenarios:** Named saved sets of input values to compare outcomes. Tools → **Scenarios** → Add → name scenario + input cells → enter values → OK. Switch scenarios from Navigator or dropdown to see different results instantly. Scenario cells get a coloured border.

**Goal Seek:** Works **backwards** – “What input gives me the result I want?” E.g. what marks needed in Term 2 to reach 90% total? Tools → **Goal Seek** → Formula cell (result cell with formula) → Target value → Variable cell (input cell to change) → OK. Calc adjusts the input automatically. (Only ONE variable cell; formula cell must contain a formula.)
**Solver:** Advanced Goal Seek – handles **multiple variable cells + constraints** (e.g. maximise profit within budget). Tools → Solver.

> 4-mark favourite: “Explain Goal Seek with steps” / “Difference between Scenario and Goal Seek” (Scenario = many inputs → see outputs; Goal Seek = fixed output → find input).

## Chapter 5: Using Macros in Spreadsheet

**Macro =** A recorded/saved sequence of actions (or small program in LibreOffice Basic) that automates repetitive tasks – one click instead of 50 steps.

**Recording a Macro:** Tools → Macros → **Record Macro** → perform actions (formatting, sorting…) → Stop Recording → save under **My Macros** → give name → Save.
**Running a Macro:** Tools → Macros → Run Macro → select library/module/macro → Run. Or assign to toolbar button/menu/shortcut/event for one-click use.
**Creating & Organising simple macro:** Tools → Macros → Organise Macros → LibreOffice Basic → My Macros → New module → write/edit code → Save. Organiser manages libraries/modules.
**Macro as a Function:** A macro written as `Function` can be used like a built-in formula, e.g. `=MYDISCOUNT(A1)` in a cell – takes arguments, returns a value.
**Passing arguments:** Values given to a macro/function in brackets, e.g. `MyMacro("Hello", 5)` – first value goes to first parameter, etc. Arguments **as values** means passing actual data (5, "A1") not just references.
**Access cells directly (code):** e.g. `ThisComponent.Sheets(0).getCellByPosition(0,0).Value = 100` writes 100 into A1 of first sheet.
**Sort columns using macro:** Record sorting steps once (select range → Data → Sort → options) → replay macro on any similar data.
**Macro security:** Tools → Options → Security → Macro Security – set Medium/High so harmful macros ask permission before running.

> ⭐ Memorise: Record path + “macro stored in My Macros” + macro-as-function concept. Practical: record a formatting macro.

## Chapter 6: Linking Spreadsheet Data

**Multiple sheets:** Insert → Sheet (or + icon / right-click sheet tab → Insert Sheet). Rename by double-clicking tab. Move/copy via right-click → Move or Copy Sheet.

**Reference to another sheet (same file):** `='Sheet2'.A1` or `=SUM(Sheet2.A1:A10)`. Create with mouse: type `=` → click other sheet tab → click cell → Enter. With keyboard: type sheet name + dot + cell manually.
**Reference to another document:** `='file:///home/user/sales.ods'#$Sheet1.A1`. Create: type `=` → switch to other open file → click cell → Enter (path inserted automatically).

**Hyperlinks:** Clickable jump to a cell/sheet/file/webpage/email. Insert → Hyperlink or **Ctrl+K** → choose Internet / Document / Mail → set target → Apply.
**Relative vs Absolute hyperlinks:** Relative stores path *relative* to current file (e.g. `../data/sales.ods`) – portable if folder moves together; Absolute stores full path/URL (e.g. `https://…` or `C:\…`) – breaks if file moves. Set default under Tools → Options → Load/Save.
**Editing a hyperlink:** Right-click link → Edit Hyperlink (or Default Paragraph to remove formatting); to remove: right-click → **Remove Hyperlink**. Note: Ctrl+Click to follow a link in Calc.
**Link to External Data:** Sheet → **Link to External Data** → enter URL/file → select named range/table → OK – data imports and refreshes (great for live prices/stock data).
**Link to Registered Data Sources:** View → Data Sources (**F4**) → registered databases (e.g. Base file registered in LibreOffice) → drag table/query into sheet. Register via Tools → Options → Base → Databases → New.

## Chapter 7: Share and Review Spreadsheet

**Sharing:** Tools → **Share Spreadsheet** → tick “Allow changes by more than one user” → OK → save on shared/network location. Title bar shows “(shared)”. Allows simultaneous editing.
**Open & Save shared:** Open normally; on save, Calc shows **Resolve Conflicts** dialog if two users changed the same cell – choose Mine/Theirs for each conflict.
**Record Changes:** Edit → Track Changes → **Record** – every edit gets a coloured border + author note.
**Comments:** Insert → Comment (**Ctrl+Alt+C**); right-click cell → Edit/Delete Comment; Show/Hide; Format via comment box borders/fonts. Used to discuss without changing values.
**Reviewing:** Edit → Track Changes → **Manage/Show** – View list of changes (author, date, old/new value) → **Accept or Reject** each (or Accept All). Filter by author/date/range.
**Merging & Comparing:** Merge: combine edited copies – Edit → Track Changes → **Merge Document** → select files. Compare: Edit → Track Changes → **Compare Document** → differences appear as tracked changes.

> ⭐ Confusion alert: Writer uses Edit → Track Changes → *Manage + Compare*; Calc adds *Merge Document + Share Spreadsheet*. Exam loves asking the difference.

# UNIT 3: Database Management System – LibreOffice Base | 12 marks (BIGGEST unit)

## Chapter 8: Introduction to DBMS

**Data** = raw facts (e.g. “Amit, 15, 92”). **Information** = processed, meaningful data (e.g. “Amit scored 92% – Grade A”).
**Database** = organised collection of related data. **DBMS** = software to create, store, manage, retrieve data (e.g. LibreOffice Base, MySQL, MS Access).
**Advantages of database over files:** No redundancy (duplication), data consistency, data integrity/accuracy, data security (passwords/rights), easy sharing, quick search/query, backup & recovery, enforces standards.
**Data Models:**
1. **Hierarchical** – tree structure, one parent per child (like organisation chart). Fast but rigid; one-to-many only.
2. **Network** – graph; a child can have many parents (many-to-many via links). Flexible but complex.
3. **Relational** – data in **tables** linked by common fields. Simple, powerful, most used (Base/MySQL/Oracle). Supports all relationship types.

**RDBMS Terminology (memorise with example – STUDENT table):**
- **Table/Relation:** Data stored as rows + columns (e.g. STUDENT).
- **Tuple / Row / Record:** One complete entry (one student’s full row).
- **Attribute / Column / Field:** One property (e.g. Name, RollNo, Marks).
- **Field value / Data item:** Single cell value (“Amit”).
- **Degree:** Number of columns/attributes. **Cardinality:** Number of rows/tuples.
- **Primary Key:** Unique + NOT NULL column identifying each row (e.g. RollNo). Only one per table.
- **Foreign Key:** Column in one table referring to Primary Key of another (links tables, e.g. ClassID in STUDENT → CLASS table).
- **Candidate Key:** All columns/combos that *could* be primary (RollNo, AdmissionNo). **Alternate Key:** Candidate keys not chosen as primary (AdmissionNo). **Composite Key:** Primary key made of 2+ columns together.
**Objects of RDBMS (4):** Tables (store data), Queries (ask questions/filter), Forms (user-friendly entry), Reports (formatted printout).

## Chapter 9: Starting with LibreOffice Base

**LibreOffice Base** = free RDBMS for tables, queries, forms, reports. Database file extension **.odb**.
**Common Data Types:** TEXT/VARCHAR (names), CHAR (fixed length), INTEGER/INT (whole numbers), DECIMAL/NUMERIC (marks, price), DATE, TIME, TIMESTAMP/DATETIME, BOOLEAN/YES-NO (true/false), MEMO/LONGVARCHAR (long remarks).
**UI:** Database pane (Tables/Queries/Forms/Reports), Tasks pane (Create Table…), main work area.
**Opening:** File → Open → select .odb (or File → New → Database → Create new / Connect to existing).
**Creating a Table – 2 ways:**
1. **Using Wizard:** Tables → Create Table in Design… no — Tables → Use Wizard to Create Table → pick category (Business/Personal) → sample table → select fields (>) → set field types → set primary key → name table → Finish. Fastest for beginners.
2. **Using Design View:** Tables → Create Table in Design View → type Field Name + Field Type for each row → set field properties (length, default, required…) → right-click a field → Primary Key → Save (Ctrl+S) → name table → OK.
**Setting Primary Key:** Design View → right-click field row → Primary Key (key icon appears). AutoNumber/AutoValue can auto-generate IDs.
**Entering data:** Double-click table → type row-wise in datasheet; Tab/Enter moves across; new blank row auto-appears. Close → saves automatically.
**Navigating:** Bottom bar – First/Previous/Next/Last record, New record, record count (e.g. “Record 3 of 50”).
**Editing:** Click cell → change value → Enter/leave row to save. **Deleting records:** Select row header → right-click/Delete key → confirm. **Sorting:** Click column header → Sort Ascending (A→Z, 0→9) / Descending button, or right-click → Sort.

## Chapter 10: Working with Multiple Tables

**Editing tables:** Right-click table → Edit (opens Design View – add/remove fields, change types). Careful: changing type may lose data.
**Deleting tables:** Right-click → Delete → confirm (deletes structure + all data permanently; fails if linked by relationships with integrity enforced).
**Relationships:** Logical links between tables via common key (primary ↔ foreign). Avoid duplication – store class details once, link everywhere.
**3 Types:**
1. **One-to-One (1:1):** One row ↔ one row. E.g. PERSON ↔ PASSPORT (one person, one passport).
2. **One-to-Many (1:N):** One row ↔ many rows. E.g. CLASS ↔ STUDENTS (one class, many students). **Most common.**
3. **Many-to-Many (M:N):** Many ↔ many. E.g. STUDENTS ↔ SUBJECTS (each student takes many subjects; each subject has many students). Implemented via a third junction table in practice.
**Advantages of relating tables:** Less redundancy, consistency, integrity, saves space, easy combined queries/reports.
**Creating relationships:** Tools → **Relationships** → drag tables into window → drag Primary Key field → drop onto matching Foreign Key field → in dialog tick **Enforce referential integrity** (+ update/delete options) → OK. Line joins the tables (1—∞ symbols).
**Referential Integrity:** Rule that foreign key must match an existing primary key (or be NULL) – prevents orphan records. E.g. can’t enter marks for RollNo 999 if that student doesn’t exist; can’t delete a CLASS still having students (or cascades as set).

## Chapter 11: Queries in Base

**Query =** A saved question asked to the database – filters/sorts/calculates data without changing stored tables. E.g. “Show names of students scoring > 80.”
**Creating with Wizard:** Queries → Use Wizard to Create Query → select table → choose fields (>) → set sorting → set search conditions → grouping/alias → name → Finish. Best for simple queries.
**Creating in Design View:** Queries → Create Query in Design View → add table(s) → grid appears: Field / Alias / Table / Sort / Visible / Criterion rows → drag fields down or pick from dropdown → set Sort (Asc/Desc) → untick Visible to hide a field → type Criterion (e.g. `>80`, `LIKE 'A*'`) → Save → Run (F5).
**Editing a query:** Right-click query → Edit → modify grid → Save → re-run.
**Criteria examples:** Single field: `Marks > 80`. Multiple fields (AND): Class='X' AND Marks>60 (same row). OR: put in different Criterion rows. **Wildcards:** `*` = many characters (`A*` → Amit, Anu), `?` = single character (`An?` → Anu, Ani). Use with LIKE in SQL.
**Numerical calculations in queries:** In Design View add Function row (View → Functions) or type in Field: `SUM(Marks)`, `AVG(Marks)`, `COUNT(*)`, `MAX`, `MIN`. SQL view: `SELECT AVG(Marks) FROM STUDENT WHERE Class='X';`

## Chapter 12: Forms and Reports

**Form =** User-friendly screen for entering/viewing/editing one record at a time (better than raw table grid; can add logos, buttons, validation).
**Creating with Wizard:** Forms → Use Wizard to Create Form → select table/query → choose fields → add subform if needed → arrange controls (columnar/tabular…) → set data entry mode → apply style → name → Finish.
**Modifying a Form:** Right-click form → Edit → drag controls to rearrange, resize, change fonts/colours, add/delete controls → Save.
**Form Controls Toolbar (View → Toolbars → Form Controls):** Label (fixed text), Text Box (type data), Button (Save/Next…), Check Box, Radio Button, List Box / Combo Box (dropdown choices), Date Field, Formatted Field. Must-have: know Label vs Text Box vs List Box.
**Enter/remove data via forms:** Open form → bottom navigator: New Record (+), Delete, Save; type into boxes → Enter. **Search:** Form toolbar → Find Record (binoculars) → type value → Search.
**Change label/background:** Edit mode → right-click control → Properties → change Label text, font, background colour/image.
**Report =** Formatted, printable output (marksheet, salary slip) – read-only, generated from tables/queries.
**Creating with Wizard:** Reports → Use Wizard to Create Report → select table/query → fields → labels → grouping levels (e.g. group by Class) → sort options → layout (tabular/columnar) + style → title → Finish/Create.
**Inserting in reports (Edit mode):** More controls (lines, images, page numbers), **Titles & Headings** (Insert → Title/Header – report name, chapter heads), **Date & Time** (Insert → Date and Time → fixed or auto-updating field; shows print date).

> ⭐ Most-asked Unit 3 Qs: Data vs Information; advantages of DBMS; 3 data models; primary vs foreign key; degree vs cardinality; 4 RDBMS objects; table creation 2 methods; 3 relationship types + example; referential integrity; query wizard vs design view; wildcard * and ?; form vs report; form controls; report wizard steps.

# UNIT 4: Maintain Healthy, Safe & Secure Working Environment | 10 marks
*(Theory-heavy + easy scoring – mostly short answers and MCQs)*

## Chapter 13: Health, Safety and Security at Workplace

**Health** = physical + mental well-being. **Safety** = protection from accidents/injury. **Security** = protection of people, data, property from theft/misuse.
**Policies & Procedures:** Written company rules – emergency exits, fire drill plan, first-aid kit location, reporting hazards, password policy, backup schedule. Everyone must know and follow them.
**Reasons for HSS programs:** Legal compliance, fewer accidents/illness, higher productivity, lower costs (medical/compensation), happy motivated staff, good company image.
**Workplace Safety Hazards:**
- **Physical:** Noise, poor lighting, extreme temperature, radiation, slippery floors
- **Falls from height:** ladders, stairs without rails; **Slips & Trips:** wet floor, loose wires, clutter
- **Electrical:** Overloaded sockets, damaged wires, no earthing → shock/fire
- **Fire:** Paper/chemicals near heat, blocked exits, no extinguisher
- **Health:** Dust/fumes causing asthma, long sitting → back pain, stress
**Potential sources:** Unsafe equipment, untrained staff, poor housekeeping, ignoring rules, overwork.
**Computer-specific hazards:** Eye strain, back/neck pain (bad posture), electric shock (open CPU/spills), data loss (no backup), e-waste.
**Handling office equipment:** Training before use, switch off + unplug before cleaning, no liquids near devices, report damage, don’t overload sockets.
**Handling objects:** Lift with knees (not back), ask help for heavy items, wear gloves for sharp/hot items, store heavy items at waist height.
**Stress at work:** Causes – deadlines, long hours, conflicts, job insecurity. Manage – breaks, exercise, talk, prioritise, realistic goals.
**Working environment:** Clean, ventilated, well-lit, right temperature, low noise, ergonomic furniture.
**Hazard control steps:** Identify → Assess risk → Control (Eliminate/Substitute/guards/training/PPE) → Review regularly.
**Safety Guidelines Checklist:** Exits clear + marked, extinguisher working, first-aid kit stocked, wires safe, backup done, passwords strong, drill practised.

## Chapter 14: Workplace Quality Measures

**Air quality monitoring:** Ventilation, no smoking indoors, AC filters cleaned, plants, exhaust fans; test for dust/CO₂ periodically.
**Water:** Clean drinking water (RO/purifier tested), no leakage/seepage, washrooms hygienic.
**Cleanliness:** Daily sweeping/mopping, dust-free desks, bins emptied + segregated (wet/dry/e-waste), pest control.
**Office Ergonomics:** Designing workplace to fit the worker – chair with back support + adjustable height, monitor at eye level ~50–70 cm away, keyboard/mouse at elbow height, feet flat on floor/footrest, frequent micro-breaks + stretching.
**Computer health & safety tips:** Correct posture, 20-20-20 rule for eyes (every 20 min, look 20 feet away for 20 sec), blink often, adjust brightness, clean screen, short breaks every hour, no eating over keyboard.
**Musculoskeletal problems & remedies:**
- **Occupational Overuse Syndrome / RSI:** Finger/wrist pain from repeat typing → ergonomic keyboard, breaks, stretch
- **Strain in legs/feet:** Long standing → footrest, sit breaks, good footwear
- **Eye strain:** Glare/long screen → anti-glare, right distance, 20-20-20
- **Headache:** Stress/posture/dehydration → breaks, water, posture fix
- **Obesity:** Sitting all day → walk/stretch, stairs, healthy food
- **Stress disorders:** Anxiety/burnout → counselling, workload balance, sleep
- **Laptop injuries:** Hunched neck → use stand + external keyboard/mouse
- **Sleeping problems:** Late-night screen → no screens 1 hr before bed, fixed sleep time
**Cautions while working on computer:** Don’t open SMPS/monitor (high voltage), unplug before servicing, don’t touch with wet hands, use stabiliser/UPS, keep liquids away, shut down properly, antivirus updated.

## Chapter 15: Prevent Accidents and Emergencies

**Accident** = sudden unplanned event causing injury/damage (fall, shock). **Emergency** = serious situation needing immediate action (fire, earthquake, medical crisis).
**5 rules when accident/emergency occurs:**
1. Notice and correctly identify what happened
2. Get help promptly in the most suitable way (call supervisor/helpline – fire 101, ambulance 102/108 in India)
3. Follow company policies to prevent further injury while waiting
4. Act only within your responsibility/authority (don’t attempt risky rescue untrained)
5. Promptly follow senior staff / emergency services instructions
**Types of accidents:** Trip & Fall (clutter/uneven floor), Slip & Fall (wet/oily floor), Escalator/Elevator injuries (trapped clothing, sudden stop), Falling goods (unstable stacking), Moving objects (trolleys, doors).
**Handling accidents:** Attend injured person immediately (don’t move spinal injury) → Inform supervisor → Assist supervisor/first-aider → record incident.
**Types of emergencies:** First Aid (medical), Electrical Safety (shock), Evacuation (fire/quake/bomb threat).
**General evacuation procedure:** Stay calm → raise alarm → switch off equipment if safe → leave by nearest safe exit (don’t use lift in fire) → assemble at assembly point → headcount → don’t re-enter till declared safe. **Practise** via mock drills; display evacuation plan map.
**Fire hazards:** Overloaded circuits, flammable paper/chemicals, kitchen/gas leaks, smoking.
**Fire prevention:** No overloading, safe storage of fuel, working alarms/extinguishers, clear exits, regular audits, staff training (PASS: Pull-Aim-Squeeze-Sweep for extinguisher).
**Material & ignition sources:** Fuel (paper, oil, gas) + Heat/spark + Oxygen = fire triangle. Remove any one side to stop fire. Identify and separate fuels from sparks/heat.
**First aid for electrical emergencies:** DON’T touch victim directly → switch off power first → separate with dry wooden stick/rubber → call help → if trained, check breathing/CPR → treat burns with clean dry cloth → shift to hospital.
**Electrical rescue techniques:** Insulate yourself (rubber gloves/shoes), use non-conductor to move wire, never use water on electrical fire (use CO₂/dry-powder extinguisher), know MCB/main switch locations.
