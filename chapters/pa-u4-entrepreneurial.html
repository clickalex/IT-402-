🎯 Why it matters

3 marks, theory-rich. “Entrepreneur vs employee” or “qualities” = classic 2-marker.

📖 Concepts (deep)

1. Definitions: Entrepreneur = person who starts/runs a business, takes risk, innovates for profit (e.g. local bakery owner, startup founder). Entrepreneurship = the process (idea → plan → launch → manage → grow). Enterprise/Business = the venture itself.

2. Entrepreneur vs Employee (exam table — learn 3 rows):

BasisEntrepreneurEmployee
Risk & incomeBears risk; uncertain profit/lossNo business risk; fixed salary
DecisionsTakes own decisionsFollows employer's instructions
WorkOwn idea, flexible hours, more responsibilityFixed role/hours, less responsibility
MotiveBuild + grow ventureCareer growth + stable income

3. Qualities of successful entrepreneur (learn 6 + 1 line): Risk-taking (calculated), leadership (guides team), decision-making (quick + firm), hard work + persistence (don't quit), innovation/creativity (new ideas), communication (convinces customers/team), goal-oriented, adaptable, honest, confident.

4. Functions of entrepreneur: Generate idea → survey market → prepare business plan → arrange money/material/men → launch → market & sell → manage accounts → bear risk → grow/innovate.

5. Myths vs Facts (very asked):

  • Myth: Entrepreneurs are born → Fact: skills can be learned/practised.
  • Myth: Need lots of money → Fact: can start small (home/cloud kitchen, tuition, online store).
  • Myth: Only for business families / only men / needs big degree → Fact: anyone with idea + effort; many first-generation + women founders.

6. Steps to start a business (6): Idea → Market survey (customers? competitors? price?) → Business plan (product, customers, cost, price, marketing, finance) → Arrange finance (savings/loan/scheme) → Set up + register (licence/shop) → Launch → Manage, review, grow.

7. Business plan contents: What product/service, who customers, competitor analysis, pricing, marketing (how to reach buyers), money needed + profit estimate, daily operations, risks + backup.

8. Risk management: Identify risks (fire, theft, no sales) → analyse impact → reduce: insurance, backup stock/cash, quality control, diversify products, written agreements.

9. Financial literacy basics: Budget (income − expense), save first, track profit = selling price − cost, avoid over-borrowing, keep bills/records, separate business + personal money.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Qualities → “RLD-HIC”: Risk, Leadership, Decision, Hard-work, Innovation, Communication.
  • Steps → “I-M-P-F-S-L”: Idea, Market-survey, Plan, Finance, Setup, Launch.

⚠️ Mistakes to avoid

  • One-sided difference (only entrepreneur's side). Always write both columns.
  • Vague “takes risk” without contrasting “fixed salary” — pair both for full marks.

❓ Exam Q&A

1-mark:
“Fixed salary person is?” → Employee. “Part of business plan?” → Pricing/marketing/finance (any).
2-mark Q:
Two qualities of entrepreneur. → Risk-taking: invests despite uncertainty (opens cloud kitchen). Communication: convinces customers/suppliers clearly.
2-mark Q:
Two myths with facts. → (1) Born entrepreneurs — false, skills learnable. (2) Needs huge money — false, can start small/home-based.

➕ Extra exam Q&A bank

1-mark: Who is an entrepreneur?
A person who starts and runs a business, takes risk and innovates for profit.
1-mark: Name any two contents of a business plan.
Product/service, target customers, pricing, marketing, finance (any two).
1-mark: State one myth and its fact about entrepreneurship.
Myth: entrepreneurs are born. Fact: entrepreneurial skills can be learned and practised.
2-mark: Differentiate entrepreneur and employee (2 points).
(1) Risk/income — entrepreneur bears risk for uncertain profit; employee gets a fixed salary. (2) Decisions — entrepreneur decides independently; employee follows instructions.
4-mark (practice): Plan a small data-entry service for local shops.
Idea: digitise paper registers into searchable Calc/Base files with weekly backup. Market survey: visit 5 shops, note record problems, check competitors' prices. Business plan: service list, pricing per 100 records, promotion via pamphlets. Finance: start small with home PC (low cost); track profit = price − cost. Risks: data privacy (take consent, restrict access), errors (double-check + checklist). Qualities used: hard work, communication, honesty, persistence. Start with one shop, collect feedback, then grow.
