🎯 Why it matters

1 fixed marker — definition or green-job example. Takes 15 minutes to master fully.

📖 Concepts (deep)

1. Environment = Our surroundings: air, water, land, plants, animals + human society. Damage = pollution, deforestation, waste, climate change.

2. Sustainable Development (Brundtland, 1987 — memorise exact line): “Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.”
Three pillars: Environment (protect nature) + Society (health, equity) + Economy (jobs, growth) — all three together.

3. Green Economy: Economic growth that is low-carbon, resource-efficient, pollution-free, socially inclusive. E.g. solar energy, e-vehicles, recycling industry, organic farming.

4. Green Jobs: Jobs protecting/restoring environment. Examples: solar panel technician, wind-turbine mechanic, energy auditor, waste-segregation manager, water-harvesting expert, organic farmer, e-vehicle mechanic, forest guard. (Non-green contrast: plastic-bag factory worker.)

5. Green Skills: Skills for green jobs — install solar panels, audit energy, manage e-waste, compost wet waste, harvest rainwater, design eco-packaging.

6. 4 Rs: Refuse (say no to plastic), Reduce (less use), Reuse (bottles/bags), Recycle (paper/e-waste to recycler).

7. School/home actions (any 4 for 2-marker): LED bulbs + switch off; save water (fix leaks, bucket bath); segregate wet/dry/e-waste bins; compost leaves/food waste; plant + maintain trees; cycle/walk/bus; double-side printing; e-notes over photocopies; clean + green drives; World Environment Day 5 June activities.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • Pillars → “ESE”: Environment-Society-Economy (“Easy”).
  • 4 Rs order → Refuse-Reduce-Reuse-Recycle (strongest → weakest action).

❓ Exam Q&A

1-mark:
“Brundtland defines?” → Sustainable development. “Green job example?” → Solar technician.
2-mark Q:
What is green economy? Give two green jobs. → Low-carbon, resource-efficient, inclusive growth model. Jobs: solar technician + e-waste manager (any valid pair).

✅ Part A Final Checklist (before exam)

➕ Extra exam Q&A bank

1-mark: Define sustainable development (Brundtland).
Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.
1-mark: Name the three pillars of sustainable development.
Environment, Society, Economy (“ESE”).
1-mark: Give two examples of green jobs.
Solar panel technician and e-waste manager (also: energy auditor, organic farmer).
2-mark: Two ways your school can go green.
(1) Switch to LED bulbs, switch off unused lights/fans and use natural light. (2) Segregate wet/dry/e-waste bins, compost leaves, print double-sided and plant trees.
4-mark (practice): What is a green economy? How can an IT student support it?
A green economy is low-carbon, resource-efficient and socially inclusive growth (solar energy, e-vehicles, recycling). An IT student can: use power-saving settings and shut down labs PCs; proofread on screen and print double-sided only when needed; send e-waste (batteries, keyboards) to authorised recyclers; make e-notes instead of photocopies; spread awareness on World Environment Day (5 June). Small daily habits protect the environment while keeping productivity high.
