Draw this 7-step loop in 2-mark answers: Sender → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback.

🎯 Why it matters

Practise the 7 Cs, barriers and feedback using workplace examples. The published 2026–27 scheme allocates 2 marks to this unit within the 10-mark Part A total; this does not guarantee a particular question.

📖 Concepts (deep)

1. What is communication? Transfer of information/ideas/feelings between sender and receiver through a medium, with feedback completing the loop.

2. Communication cycle (7 steps) — draw this in 2-marker:
Sender → Encoding (converting thought into words/gestures) → Message → Channel/Medium (phone, letter, face-to-face) → Decoding (receiver interprets) → Receiver → Feedback (reply/confirmation).
Example: Teacher explains (sender/encoding) → speaks in class (message/channel) → student understands (decoding) → nods/asks doubt (feedback).

3. Methods of communication:

MethodMeansExample
Verbal – OralSpoken wordsPhone call, speech, meeting
Verbal – WrittenWritten wordsEmail, letter, report, SMS
Non-verbalBody, voice, touchSmile, handshake, eye contact, tone, posture
VisualPictures/signsCharts, maps, traffic signals, logos

4. 7 Cs of effective communication (memorise with example):

  1. Clear – simple words, one idea at a time (“Submit file by Friday” not “do it soon-ish”)
  2. Concise – short, no extra words
  3. Concrete – specific facts/figures (“sales rose 20%” not “sales rose a lot”)
  4. Correct – accurate grammar, facts, spelling
  5. Coherent – logical order, connected sentences
  6. Complete – all needed info (what, when, where, whom)
  7. Courteous – polite, respectful (“please”, “thank you”)

5. Barriers (with one-line example each):

  • Physical – noise, distance, faulty phone
  • Linguistic/Language – different languages, jargon (“client speaks French, manual in English”)
  • Cultural – different customs/gestures meanings
  • Emotional – anger/fear blocks listening
  • Perceptual – different viewpoints/assumptions
  • Organisational – strict hierarchy, no feedback channel
  • Interpersonal – poor relationship, distrust, interruptions

6. Feedback: Receiver's response showing message was understood. Good feedback is descriptive (says what happened), specific, timely, constructive (suggests improvement, not insults).
Example: ❌ “Bad presentation.” ✅ “Your data was good; add bigger fonts on slide 3 and slow down.”

7. Principles of effective communication: Clarity, completeness, attention (listen fully), consistency (words match actions), timeliness, proper channel, feedback, courtesy.

8. Speaking skills: Correct grammar, clear pronunciation, fluency (smooth flow), rich vocabulary, right speed + voice modulation (stress/pause/tone).

9. Overcoming stage fear (public speaking): Know your topic, prepare notes, practise aloud/in mirror, deep breathing, start with smile + greeting, make eye contact, use simple slides, interact (ask questions).

10. Active listening (DOs): Face speaker, eye contact, nod, don't interrupt, paraphrase (“So you mean…?”), take notes, ask clarifying questions.

🧠 Memory tricks

  • 7 Cs → "Can Cows Chew Cold Corn Cob Candy?" (Clear Concise Concrete Correct Coherent Complete Courteous)
  • Cycle order → SEMC DRF (“Seema Drink RF”): Sender-Encoding-Message-Channel-Decoding-Receiver-Feedback

⚠️ Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing only “verbal/non-verbal” without oral/written split when asked for methods.
  • Confusing Encoding (sender side) vs Decoding (receiver side).
  • Forgetting example in 2-marker — example = half marks.

❓ Exam Q&A

1-mark:
“French manual” barrier → Linguistic. “7th C starting with Co…” → Courteous/Coherent/Concrete/Complete/Correct (any).
2-mark Q:
Explain any two Cs. → Pick Clear + Concise/Courteous, 1 line + 1 mini-example each.
2-mark Q:
What is feedback? Why important? → Response showing understanding; confirms message, corrects errors, improves performance; must be specific + timely + constructive.

💻 Practical (for file/viva)

Write a formal email to principal requesting leave (Subject + greeting + 3-line body + sign-off). Demonstrates written communication + email etiquette.

➕ Extra exam Q&A bank

1-mark: Expand the 7 Cs (any four).
Clear, Concise, Concrete, Correct, Coherent, Complete, Courteous.
1-mark: Encoding happens on whose side — sender or receiver?
Sender's side (converting thought into words/gestures); decoding is on the receiver's side.
1-mark: Give one example each of oral and written communication.
Oral: phone call / meeting. Written: email / report.
2-mark: Explain two barriers to communication with one example each.
(1) Linguistic barrier — people use different languages, e.g. a client cannot read a French manual. (2) Physical barrier — noise or distance blocks the message, e.g. a faulty phone line during an order call.
4-mark (practice): Describe the communication cycle with a data-entry example and the role of feedback.
Sender encodes an idea into a message and sends it through a channel; the receiver decodes it and returns feedback. Example: a supervisor dictates a customer record (sender/encoding) over phone (channel); the operator repeats the spelling (decoding + feedback). Feedback confirms understanding, corrects errors early and improves accuracy. Good feedback is descriptive, specific, timely and constructive — e.g. “Row 4's phone number has 9 digits; please recheck” instead of “wrong data”.
