Chapter 1: Communication Skills-II

Learn how workplace messages are sent, received and confirmed — then answer exam questions and practise like a Data Entry Operator.

📖 Part 1 · Detailed notes❓ Part 2 · Questions💻 Part 3 · Practice
After this chapter you can:
  • Explain the communication cycle and the importance of feedback.
  • Compare verbal, non-verbal and visual/written methods with examples.
  • Identify barriers and apply measures to overcome them.
  • Apply the 7 Cs and write clear formal sentences.
On this page: Details Questions Practice Checklist

📖 Part 1 · Detailed chapter

🎯 Marks lens (2 marks): communication cycle, feedback types, barriers + measures, 7 Cs and one formal-writing example. Examiners reward correct terms + a workplace example.

1. What is communication?

Communication is the exchange of ideas so the receiver understands the intended meaning. In a data-entry office it happens with customers, supervisors and teammates — in person, on phone, on email and inside documents.

Sender
encodes idea→Message + Medium
words / call / email→Receiver
decodes meaning→Feedback
confirms it

Noise is anything that distorts the message: loud room, poor network, difficult words, stress or wrong assumptions.

2. Methods of communication

MethodWhat it isStrengthsWeaknesses
Verbal (spoken)Face-to-face talk, phone, voice noteFast, tone + instant questionsNo record; forgotten details
Non-verbalPosture, eye contact, gestures, facial expressionBuilds trust, supports wordsEasy to misread across cultures
Written / visualEmail, letter, report, chart, noticePermanent record, exact numbersSlow; tone can be misunderstood
Workplace example: confirm a customer name spelling verbally on the call, then send it back in a written email so there is a record.
Do's & don'ts — body language: sit straight, keep eye contact, nod to show attention. Don't cross arms, look at the phone, interrupt or use slang with customers.

3. Communication cycle & importance of feedback

  1. Sender has an idea and encodes it into words/symbols.
  2. Message travels through a medium (talk, email, form).
  3. Receiver decodes it using language and context.
  4. Feedback returns — confirming, correcting or asking more.
  5. Both sides adjust until meaning matches.
Feedback typeMeaningExample
DescriptiveExplains what was observed"You entered 19 of 20 records; row 7 has a blank phone field."
SpecificPoints to exact behaviour + effect"Because row 7 is blank, the report total is wrong. Please recheck."
Non-specific ❌Vague praise/blame, not actionable"Good job" / "This is bad"
🧠 Memory trick: S–M–M–R–F — Sender → Message → Medium → Receiver → Feedback.

4. Barriers to effective communication + measures

Barrier typeExamplesHow to overcome
PhysicalNoise, distance, poor network, small fontQuiet place, clear audio, readable format
LinguisticDifficult words, jargon, different languageSimple words, explain terms, confirm spelling
InterpersonalNo trust, fear, anger, judging earlyListen patiently, be polite, don't interrupt
OrganisationalNo clear channel, too many levelsUse the correct channel; keep records
CulturalDifferent customs, gestures, expectationsRespect differences, ask politely
❌ Barrier in actionOperator assumes "tomorrow" means morning and misses the 4 pm deadline.
✅ Measure appliedOperator asks: "Do you mean tomorrow before 4 pm? I will confirm by email."

5. Principles of effective communication — the 7 Cs

CMeaningExample sentence
ClearOne idea, simple words"Please send the March bill register."
ConciseShort, no extra words"Meeting at 10 am, Room 2."
ConcreteExact facts + numbers"20 records, zero blanks, by 4 pm."
CorrectRight spelling, grammar, factsCheck name, date, total before sending
CoherentLogical orderSubject → point 1 → point 2 → action
CompleteAll needed detailsWho, what, when, where, attachment
CourteousRespectful tone"Thank you. Please confirm…"

6. Basic writing skills

Parts of speech (quick): noun (record), pronoun (it), verb (verify), adjective (correct), adverb (carefully), preposition (in), conjunction (and), interjection (oh!).

Sentence essentials: subject + verb + object, one idea per sentence, polite formal words.

Formal email frame: Subject → Greeting (Respected Sir/Ma'am) → Purpose in line 1 → 2–3 short points → Action + deadline → Thanking you → Name, Class, Roll No.
⚠️ Common mistakes: silence ≠ agreement; slang in formal mail; sending without proofreading names/numbers; vague feedback like "okay"; sharing OTPs or private data.

❓ Part 2 · Questions

Quick self-test (tap an option)

Score: 0/0 answered

1. Which part of the cycle confirms understanding?

2. "Row 7 phone field is blank, so the total is wrong" is…

3. "Thank you. Please confirm the spelling" shows which C?

One-mark questions

1. What is communication?
The exchange of ideas so the receiver understands the intended meaning.
2. Name the five elements of the communication cycle.
Sender, message, medium, receiver and feedback.
3. What is feedback?
The receiver's response that confirms or corrects understanding.
4. Give one example of non-verbal communication.
Eye contact, posture, gesture or facial expression.
5. What is noise?
Anything that distorts a message, e.g. loud sound or poor network.
6. Expand one C: what does "Concrete" mean?
Giving exact facts and numbers instead of vague words.

Two-mark questions

1. Specific vs non-specific feedback?
Specific feedback names the exact behaviour and effect ("row 7 blank → total wrong"); non-specific feedback is vague ("good/bad") and not actionable.
2. State two barriers and one measure for each.
Noise → choose a quiet place; difficult words → use simple language and confirm meaning.
3. Why is written communication needed in data entry?
It keeps a permanent record of exact names, numbers and instructions that can be rechecked.
4. Give two active-listening behaviours.
Do not interrupt; maintain attention; ask a clarifying question and paraphrase.

Four-mark model answer

Explain the communication cycle with a data-entry example. (4)
A sender encodes an idea into a message and selects a medium. The receiver decodes it and returns feedback. Noise such as poor language or a bad connection can distort meaning. Example: a customer dictates an email on call; the operator repeats the spelling (feedback), confirms by email (written record) and saves only after confirmation — closing the cycle.
Apply the 7 Cs to a short office message. (4)
Write one clear purpose, keep it concise, add concrete numbers ("20 records by 4 pm"), keep facts correct, order points coherently, include all needed details (who/what/when/attachment) and stay courteous ("Thank you. Please confirm.").

💻 Part 3 · Practice

Guided activities (15–20 min)

  1. Pros & cons table: on paper/Writer, make 3 rows (verbal, non-verbal, written) and write 2 pros + 2 cons of each from office life.
  2. Feedback rewrite: convert "This data is wrong" into one descriptive + one specific feedback sentence.
  3. Barrier hunt: take a confusing WhatsApp/class message, name its barrier type and rewrite it using the 7 Cs.
  4. Formal mail drill: draft a 6-line mail to your teacher: subject, greeting, purpose, 2 points, action + thanks.
